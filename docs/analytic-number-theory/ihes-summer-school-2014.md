## The mathematical landscape

The school moves across both classical and newer parts of analytic number theory, with a strong focus on prime numbers. Its main themes include:

<div class="course-topic-grid">
  <div class="course-topic"><strong>Sieve methods</strong><span>Prime gaps, refinements, and asymptotic sieve ideas</span></div>
  <div class="course-topic"><strong>Arithmetic progressions</strong><span>Distribution questions beyond the direct reach of the Riemann Hypothesis</span></div>
  <div class="course-topic"><strong>Trace functions</strong><span>Exponential sums over finite fields and their analytic applications</span></div>
  <div class="course-topic"><strong>Modular forms and \(L\)-functions</strong><span>Spectral and Langlands flavored analytic number theory</span></div>
  <div class="course-topic"><strong>Additive combinatorics</strong><span>Combinatorial structure inside arithmetic problems</span></div>
</div>

## How I plan to study it

1. Map the lecture before getting lost in details.
2. Fix the notation and isolate the main theorem or question.
3. Reconstruct the key calculations without skipping steps.
4. Separate the proof mechanism from the technical estimates.
5. Add references, connections, and questions that remain open for me.
6. Upload the finished English notes lecture by lecture.
